Data and methods

Both tests use complete responses from the cleaned Canvas dataset and a .05 significance level. Five-point ratings are treated as approximately interval. The data source was Case 31 Data_efcu_4.sav.

H1 | Account awareness and service adequacy

Service adequacy differed by awareness group (F(2, 125) = 4.63, p = .011, η² = .069). Account users gave the lowest rating (3.08 out of 5). They scored 0.55 points below unaware members (Tukey-adjusted p = .017) and 0.64 below aware nonusers (adjusted p = .011). The latter two groups did not differ significantly (adjusted p = .782). Residuals departed from normality, and the Kruskal–Wallis check also found a group difference (p = .029).

H2 | Borrowing rates and agreement that rates are lower

Descriptive statistics and paired-samples t-test

A paired t-test compares each member’s borrowing-rate rating (Q8r) with their agreement that credit union rates are lower than competitors’ (Q30). Responses marked “no opinion” were excluded.

Difference 95% CI lower 95% CI upper t df p
Assigned coding 0.084 -0.193 0.362 0.601 106 0.549
Direction-aligned coding -0.196 -0.362 -0.031 -2.353 106 0.020

With the assigned coding, the mean paired difference was 0.08 points (95% CI −0.19 to 0.36, t(106) = 0.60, p = .549). The data provide insufficient evidence of a difference.

Higher Q8r scores mean higher perceived rates. Higher Q30 scores mean stronger agreement that rates are lower than competitors’. Reversing Q8r to align the directions gave a mean difference of −0.20 points (p = .020).
